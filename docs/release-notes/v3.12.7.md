# jawata-studio v3.12.7 — a broken workspace shows up in seconds, not minutes

v3.12.5 taught the Dashboard to say **cannot be read** when a resident reports
that it can no longer read its workspace. It was right, and it was up to five
minutes late.

## Why it was slow

The studio asks each resident two real questions to prove its engine still
answers — a knowledge lookup and a compiler lookup. Those cost something, so
they are spaced five minutes apart while everything looks well.

Whether a resident can read its own workspace was riding along with them. It
should not have been. That answer is one the resident already keeps current
and simply hands over: **about 20 milliseconds, measured against a 29-project
workspace**, connection and process startup included. There was nothing to
protect it from.

## What changed

That one cheap question now runs on its own five-second timer, and it repaints
the tray as well as the row — so an unreadable workspace turns the tray amber
within seconds, not at the next deep round.

The deep check keeps its own rhythm, unchanged.

## The one asymmetry, on purpose

The fast path may only ever **raise** the alarm. A workspace that has recovered
is not cleared by it, because the green verdict also rests on the two questions
this path never asks, and restoring it would be answering them without having
asked. Recovery is prompt regardless: once anything is not green, the deep
check runs every fifteen seconds rather than every five minutes.

A round in which every workspace is still readable publishes nothing at all,
which is what makes a five-second cadence free on a healthy machine.
