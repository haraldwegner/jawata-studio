# v4.1.4 — the release-count gate goes; a fix is judged by its lineage

No binary behaviour changes in this release. It changes two seat texts and
deletes one dev-machine script, on Harald's ruling of 2026-09-05, an hour after
v4.1.3 shipped over that script's refusal.

## What was wrong

`build/patch-streak-gate.sh` refused a patch release when another patch on the
same major.minor had landed inside seven days, and only a `STREAK_OVERRIDE`
value Harald set could clear it. Its stated purpose was rule 12 of the architect
seat — a run of fixes where each one breaks something different is one defect
being moved around, and the answer is an architecture run, not a third fix.
Its predicate was release density. His four objections, in his order:

1. jawata is a product with users; counting releases is not a product concern,
   and a fix that is ready ships.
2. The rule is about patches on patches, where the next patch breaks something
   else — an architecture smell that wants an architecture run, not a refusal.
3. The codebase is large; a patch can be far from the previous one, and a
   count carries no locality.
4. A week is the wrong clock when whole sprints happen in a day, and the
   product is live between the patches — dogfood then patch is the process
   working, not a streak.

Two facts about the gate confirmed the reading. It fired on the SECOND patch on
a minor, not the third, so at this cadence it fired on nearly every patch. And
on v4.1.3 the architect had already read the whole run and ruled design fix
before the gate fired — the action the gate prescribed could not discharge it,
only his word could, which made it a formality.

## What changed

- **The script is deleted.** Nothing runs it any more; the CI copy had already
  gone on 2026-09-02 on his ruling that a patch-count gate is dev hygiene, not
  CI. The release workflow's header comment says so instead of pointing at a
  file that no longer exists.
- **Architect seat, rule 12, keeps the alarm and names its unit.** His ruling is
  quoted: *"The rule is to prevent patching and getting a rotten design and not
  to prevent patches at all. Dogfood and patching different modules makes
  sense. What does not make sense is to move an error in the same bugfix
  session from one place to another, because this rots the design or is a smell
  for a bad architectural design already."* His follow-up question fixed the
  unit: what does the same session mean when a release and a rollout lie
  between two fixes? Nothing about a defect resets at a tag, so the unit is the
  defect's LINEAGE, read from the code: blame the lines a fix is about to
  change and the path the failure runs through; if their last change was
  itself a fix, this fix is fixing a fix, and the chain — across any number of
  releases — is the architect's target. A patch after dogfood is the process
  working; two patches in unrelated modules are two fixes; a fix landing on a
  previous fix's lines is the alarm. Blame over-fires where those lines are
  simply where the behaviour lives, and the architect, not the blame, says
  whether the faces share a structure.
- **Debugger seat: blame before the probe.** The seat gains a step between
  recall and the discriminating observation — blame the failing path; if its
  last change was a fix, hand the chain to the architect before writing
  anything. This is the moment the deleted gate missed: it asked its question
  at tagging, when the fix was already written and tested, and the only useful
  time to ask is when the answer can still change what gets written.

## What did not change

The hook binary and the studio binary are rebuilt at the new version and are
otherwise identical to v4.1.3. The blame check is seat text — a reader's step,
not a hook. A hook would have to classify a commit as a fix, and the only
mechanical proxy (tag lineage) fires on every edit to recently patched code;
the judgement the alarm needs sits in the architect anyway. If the seat text
turns out not to fire, a hook is the next rung, and the record will show a fix
chain nobody read.

The installed `/refactor` and `/debug` skills are generated from the seat files
by the studio on deploy, so they carry the old text until this release deploys.

## Gates

`cargo test --release` over both crates in `src-tauri`, the CI step, green on
the bumped tree; the attribution audit over the unpushed range clean.
