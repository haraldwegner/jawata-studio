# v4.1.3 — armed means pending, and the judge is asked every time

Found by the sleep of 2026-09-05. Under a granted autocontinue the agent built
four rows of a plan between 01:24 and 03:13, posted a summary ending
"Continuing there", made no tool call, and the session slept **4h 13m 52s**
until Harald typed "Status?". The stop gate fired 43 ms after the summary and
allowed the stop. Its own record of that decision, in `outcomes.log`:

    allow · autonomy=Granted empty=0 worked=true armed=true bounced=true

`armed=true` was the whole failure, and it was measured rather than reasoned:
the window held four sharded-suite runs launched in the background between
01:41 and 02:47, and every one had finished — the transcript holds their
completions as `queue-operation` records at 01:50, 02:16, 02:36 and 02:56,
each carrying the launching tool-use id. The gate never opened that record
type, so "launched in the background" read as "still running" for as long as
the window lasted. Its own header excused this in so many words: *a launch
proves only that something once started, it may already have finished, so the
gate blocks only on the direction it can prove.*

## What changed — Harald's five points, 2026-09-05

**1. The phrase list is gone.** The gate computed three detectors over prose on
every stop: a forty-phrase ask detector (`YOUR WORD`, `SHALL I`, `AWAITING`,
`BLOCKED ON YOU`…), a `DECISION:` line detector, and a question-or-imperative
detector over his own message. Not one of them had been read by any rule in
`judge` since v4.0.0; they were computed, tested, and consulted by nothing — the
retired rule's reader surviving the retirement, which is this crate's recorded
way of shipping a fix to one copy of a pair. His ruling on why they must not
come back: every phrase is an appeal to a HUMAN reader, meaningless when another
agent drives the session, and a list keyed on the agent's own wording fails open
because the party it constrains writes the text it matches. Deleted, with the
tests that pinned them; what replaces them is one test asserting that the
wording of a stop changes nothing about its verdict.

**2. The grant check stays.** AUTOCONTINUE on or off, as `autonomy.rs` records
it from his keyboard. Untouched.

**3. Armed means pending.** A launch arms the turn only while two things hold:
it was backgrounded, and the harness has not reported it finished.

- *Backgrounded* is read from the call's `run_in_background` flag AND from the
  harness's own answer to the call — `Async agent launched successfully`,
  `Command running in background with ID:` — because a client that backgrounds
  subagents by default puts no flag on the call. Measured over this session's
  transcript: 477 of 767 background agent launches carried no flag. A
  foreground `Agent` spawn, whose report came back inline, arms nothing now; it
  used to arm the turn whatever its flags.
- *Finished* is read from every record the harness writes it in: a task
  notification arriving as a user line, a `queue-operation` (the shape a
  completion takes when it is absorbed mid-turn — 977 of 2139 background
  launches in this session finished that way, all four suite runs of the 03:13
  window among them), a `queued_command` attachment, and a `task_status`
  attachment naming the task id. Matched by tool-use id or by the task id read
  off the launch result; a `SendMessage` to a finished agent re-arms it.
- Pending launches are **carried across window resets**. A suite started three
  windows ago and still running is still going to wake the session; until now
  it was forgotten at the first human line or notification, so Rule B could push
  a turn that had a job pending. A scheduled wake (`ScheduleWakeup` without
  `stop`) or a cron arms the window that made it and only that window — its
  firing is the next user line and it leaves no completion record, so carried
  it would read as pending forever.
- The `stop-verdict` log line now names what is pending
  (`pending=Bash:ba78fhp8z,…`), so the next 03:13 does not need a byte-offset
  dig to learn what the gate thought was armed.

**4. One rule, one cell.** Grant ON and nothing pending → the agent is told to
spawn the judge, every time, and the judge alone decides RESERVED or
RESOLVABLE. A verdict now belongs to ONE ATTEMPT — the span between two of the
gate's own pushes — and the first verdict of an attempt governs it: a second
spawn inside the same attempt is a re-roll and is ignored. The judge's stance
gains a paragraph for being consulted after it has spoken: if its last
instruction was carried out, judge the new state; if not, repeat it word for
word. A judge run in the background reports through the wake-up it causes, and
its verdict is read out of that notification and survives the window it opens.

**5. The "acted on" path is gone.** v4.1.0's `THE JUDGE'S LAST VERDICT HAS BEEN
ACTED ON` arm made the gate remember whether work had happened since a verdict.
It is subsumed by 4: the block that carried a RESOLVABLE instruction was itself
a push, the push ended the attempt, and the next stop consults the judge afresh
by construction. The judge reads the transcript; the gate has no reason to
remember what the judge can see. The three claims that arm protected are pinned
by the rewritten test: told and idle → held with the same instruction and the
idle valve stands down; pushed then worked → a fresh consultation, never the
stale message; a fresh verdict after the push governs.

## The architect's watch over the run, before the tag

The dev-machine patch-streak gate refused this tag — v4.1.1 and v4.1.2 landed
the day before — and its prescribed action is an architect watch over the WHOLE
run, not another fix. A fresh-context architect seat read v4.0.0 through this
tree and ruled:

> **STREAK VERDICT: ONE STRUCTURE** — `read_turn` reconstructing session state
> from a transcript the harness writes for another purpose, one unread record
> shape at a time: v4.0.0 (the agent's own text as authority) → v4.0.2 (the
> verdict not bound to its call id) → v4.1.0 (a lifetime inferred from work) →
> v4.1.2 (`isSidechain` unread) → v4.1.3 (`queue-operation`, `attachment` and
> the launch-answer sentences unread). v4.1.1 touched no code and is not a
> member; the mechanical gate counted it, the seat's own test excludes it.

On this release it ruled **design fix on the predicate** — armed-means-pending
reads facts the harness writes, and removing the remembered lifetime and the
unread detectors leaves fewer inferences and fewer faces — and named three
findings, all taken before the tag:

- **F1, fail-open (fixed).** The first cut matched the harness's launch
  sentence with `contains`, so a foreground call whose OUTPUT merely quoted it —
  a transcript dig, the judge citing what it read — was marked backgrounded,
  never completed, carried across every window: Rule B disarmed for the rest
  of the session. Measured in this session's own transcript: 1800 genuine
  answers all BEGIN with the sentence (10 for unflagged Bash calls, so the
  sentence must still be read), six results quote it mid-text, all foreground,
  two of them the judge's. The match is now anchored at the start of the
  result's text, with a subagent's block-shaped result read block by block. A
  test pins the quoting call, the judge's citation, and the genuine answer as
  its control.
- **F2, a narrowing the notes had claimed as pinned (fixed).** The idle valve's
  exemption for "told what to do, doing nothing" lived on the verdict, and the
  verdict now dies at the push — so told, pushed, idle, pushed, idle would have
  RELEASED the turn after two empty attempts: a sleep wearing the wedge valve's
  clothes. The gate now keeps one bit past the push, `instruction_open`: set by
  a RESOLVABLE verdict, cleared by the next real tool call, deciding no stop —
  it only keeps the valve shut. Pinned both ways: idle at the ceiling with an
  open instruction is held; idle at the ceiling after working is released.
- **F3 (recorded).** Commit `f737849`, *Round one is the full audit; every later
  round is scoped to the repairs* — 21 lines in `skills/sprint.md`, committed
  2026-09-04 and unpushed — ships in this tag and is named here.

**Cleared by Harald, 2026-09-05.** The gate stayed red after the watch, as it is
designed to — only he clears a streak — and his word was *Release!*. The override
value handed to the gate quotes that word, so its provenance is in the gate's own
output as well as here.

The channel question the architect has raised before stands unmade: the gate
still RECOVERS what is pending from the transcript rather than being TOLD by
the harness, because no task registry with status exists on disk. Within that
channel this is the best reading available.

## What did not change

`refactoring`-style exclusions of the communicator and the judge from "armed"
are removed rather than kept: with completion in view they are redundant — a
foreground consultation arms nothing because it has answered, a background one
is genuinely pending and its answer wakes the session. Both are still NOT work,
so they still do not reset the emptiness ceiling.

The legacy script generation in `manager_service.rs` — the stop hook's fallback
for an install whose bundle shipped no sidecar — still carries its own ask
detector. It is deployed nowhere the binary is present; it is named here so the
list of phrase lists is complete, and it is not this release's change.
